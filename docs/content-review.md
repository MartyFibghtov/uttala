# Pronunciation content review

Scope: src/lib/sounds.ts (sv, en), src/lib/minimal-pairs.ts (sv, en), Swedish sentences in src/lib/languages.ts and src/lib/course.ts.

## Sources
- Wikipedia, "Swedish phonology" (length complementarity, retroflexion, ö lowering before r, u/y IPA, pitch-accent pairs anden/tomten/buren and contours).
- Standard knowledge of Swedish orthography (SAOL-style spelling rules for sj/tj) and English phonology (Cambridge/Oxford dictionary IPA).

## Checked and confirmed
- Swedish: IPA for sj (ɧ), tj (ɕ), retroflexes, y, ö, u; sj/tj spelling notes (sk/k before front vowels, -tion, stj, skj, kj); all practice words contain the stated sound; glas/glass, vit/vitt, tak/tack, ful/full are real words differing in vowel/consonant length; all Swedish sentences and translations are grammatical and correct.
- English: all 28 sound entries (IPA, practice words, extras) and all 18 minimal pairs are correct.

## Changed
- sv-pitch: replaced the inaccurate claim that "many words carry a second rise" with accent 1 / accent 2 description (accent 2 = two peaks); added tomten and buren to the anden example.
- sv-o (ö): "mid e as in bed" changed to close-mid e (German "Tee"); added lowering before r (göra, höra).
- en-ai: removed the dubious "cutting the glide short before voiceless consonants" mistake (that is natural English); replaced with dropping the glide.

## For a native speaker to glance at
- sv-sj "round your lips" wording: sj realisation varies by region; description is deliberately general.
- sv-u "start from ee, then pout": ʉː is central, compressed rather than a pure rounded front vowel; teaching shorthand only.
- Pitch-accent contour wording (Wikipedia's Stockholm description is internally inconsistent); examples are solid, contour description is simplified.
- "Jag vill ha en kaffe" is fine; "ett kaffe" also occurs.
- Practice-word "kärlek" (tj-sound) also contains retroflex rl; fine, but check it is the intended example.
